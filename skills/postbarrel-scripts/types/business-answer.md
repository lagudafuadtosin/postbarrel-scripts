# Answer a question or review

What this is: a spoken piece answering a question customers actually ask, or responding to real feedback, read by the owner over their own footage — plain and human, never corporate PR.

## The interview

These are the questions behind the three-message interview in SKILL.md. Fold them into those messages in plain words. Optional ones can be skipped.

1. What's the question or the comment you're responding to, in their exact words if it's a review?
   Hint for them: The actual FAQ people ask, or the real review/feedback. Keep a reviewer's wording as it was.
2. What's your honest answer?
   Hint for them: Straight and human. Including 'fair point' if it is one.
3. Why is that the answer, and the real reason behind it? (optional)
   Hint for them: The reason that makes the answer trustworthy: a constraint, a choice, a thing people don't see.
4. Anything else you want to add? More detail, another angle, anything you didn't get to? (optional)
   Hint for them: Optional: Fill, if you've got more to say. Skip, if you don't.

What a strong script needs from these answers: one real question or comment (the customer's own words if it's a review), the owner's honest answer, and the real reason behind that answer. The most trustworthy version concedes what's fair before it pushes back, and never hides behind non-answers. Defensive corporate language — 'we take all feedback seriously', 'your call is important to us' — is the failure mode; a plain, human answer that says 'yes, that's fair' when it is, or 'here's why we do it this way' when there's a real reason, is the point.

How to judge the answers: The most common gap is a non-answer — a defensive deflection instead of an actual response. Push for the real answer, the one they'd give a regular across the counter. If it's a review response, get the reviewer's actual wording and whether the criticism has a fair part; conceding the fair part is what makes the rest land. The real reason behind the answer (a constraint, a deliberate choice, a thing customers don't see) is usually the strongest material — surface it. Register (straight answer, fair point, common myth, with respect) drives delivery — a genuine FAQ and a pointed review need completely different temperatures.

## Research

Research is OFF for this type. Nothing can be verified, so write only from the person's answers.

## Script structure

Build the script through these beats, in this order. Each rule is a requirement. "Fails if" names the mistake to avoid.

**the_question**
- State the question or comment plainly and fairly — a reviewer's own words kept where given, never softened or twisted to be easier to answer
- Fails if: It misrepresents the question, straw-mans a reviewer, or buries the actual question under preamble

**the_answer** (peak beat)
- The honest answer, landed clearly — conceding what's fair before pushing back where there's a real disagreement
- If the material is weak here: If the answer is simply 'yes' or 'no', say it plainly and let the reason carry the rest, rather than padding it into corporate reassurance
- Fails if: It gives a defensive non-answer, hides behind PR language, or refuses to concede a fair point the person's own answer conceded

**the_why**
- The real reason behind the answer — the constraint, the deliberate choice, or the thing customers don't see that makes it make sense
- If the material is weak here: If no reason was given, stop at the plain answer rather than inventing a justification
- Fails if: It invents a reason the person didn't give, or dresses a limitation up as a virtue the answers don't support

**close**
- A plain landing in the owner's register — the answer stands on its own, no defensive spin and no sudden sell
- Fails if: It ends on a defensive flourish, a guilt-trip at the reviewer, or a call to action the question never called for

Ordering exception: If the answer is a flat concession — 'yes, that's fair' — it can open on the concession and let the reason follow, rather than restating the whole complaint first.

Peak moment check: look at the person's own words at the "the_answer" beat, wherever it ends up. If they carry real intensity (repetition, an absolute, a superlative, weight), mark it with exactly ONE of the three peak techniques in engine-rules.md, and nowhere else. If their words there are plain, use none.

## Delivery registers

Read the person's emotional register from their own words, pick the one that matches, and apply its mechanics throughout.

**STRAIGHT ANSWER / HERE'S THE ACTUAL ANSWER**: A genuine FAQ — a real question with a real, uncomplicated answer
- State the question, then answer it in as few words as it takes.
- The reason follows plainly, doing the work reassurance would otherwise fake.
- No hedging, no 'it depends' where the person gave a real answer.
- Ends on the plain answer, nothing added.

**FAIR POINT / YEAH, THAT'S ON US**: Feedback with a genuinely valid criticism the person is willing to own
- Concede the fair part first, plainly and without a 'but' rushing in.
- Only then, the reason or the context — never as an excuse that undoes the concession.
- Keep it at the person's own temperature; owning it is the whole move.
- Ends level, on what's true, no attempt to win.

**COMMON MYTH / PEOPLE ALWAYS THINK THIS**: A recurring misconception the owner wants to correct
- Name the misconception in the customer's own framing, fairly.
- Correct it with the real fact or reason, stated once and cleanly.
- No condescension — the myth is common because it's reasonable to believe.
- Ends on the corrected fact, plainly.

**WITH RESPECT / I HEAR YOU, BUT**: An unfair or mistaken review the owner wants to answer firmly but politely
- Restate the review fairly, keeping the reviewer's dignity intact.
- Push back on the specific thing that's wrong, with the real reason, never with heat.
- Concede any genuinely fair fragment before disputing the rest.
- Ends firm and calm — the position stated, the door left open, no last word aimed to wound.

## Voice signature (always, on top of the register)

- The question or review restated in its own words before it's answered — fairly, never softened into something easier to beat — so the answer is clearly answering the real thing and not a convenient version of it. A reviewer's wording kept as given.
- The fair part conceded first, flatly and without a 'but' arriving to undo it — 'yeah, that's on us', 'you're right, that queue was too long' — because owning what's true is what earns the right to explain the rest. Only a concession the person's own answer actually made.
- The real reason as the spine — a constraint, a deliberate trade-off, a thing customers don't see — stated plainly rather than dressed up as a virtue. Only the reason the person gave; never a justification invented to make a limitation sound chosen.
- No reputation-management vocabulary — 'we value your feedback', 'we're passionate about', 'your experience matters to us' are banned; the answer is said the way the owner would say it to a regular, in their own plain words.
- Heat kept out of a pushback — a firm disagreement delivered calm, at the person's own temperature, never a last line aimed to wound the reviewer. Only ever as firm as the person's own answer was.

## Required in every script

the script must sound like a real person answering honestly, not a brand managing its reputation — the actual question stated fairly, a straight answer that concedes what's fair, and a real reason where one exists. Defensive non-answers and PR language are the failure mode; plainness, including 'that's a fair point', is the trust.

## Hard rules for this type, above all others

- Never misrepresent, twist, or straw-man the question or the reviewer's words — respond to the real thing, in their framing, or the whole piece reads as evasion.
- Never disparage, mock, or identify the reviewer beyond what the person gave — first name or 'a customer' only, and never a detail that could identify them.
- Never state a policy, refund, guarantee, or commitment beyond exactly what the person's own answers gave — an answer that promises something the owner didn't say is worse than no answer.
- Never make a claim with regulated weight (safety, health, legal, certification) in the answer unless the person stated it; research is disabled and nothing here can be verified.
- Keep any disagreement at the person's own temperature — never escalate a calm owner into a combative one, and never write a response that could read as defamatory about the reviewer.

If one of these rules requires a refusal, refuse, however rich the material is. Say plainly what you cannot write and why.

## Business details

Before the interview, ask once for a short description of the business (what it is, where, what it sells). Treat it as established fact about who they are. Reuse it for later scripts in the same chat.

Use from the Moves list in engine-rules.md: 4, 8, 9. Only where their material supports them.
