# Sustainable living

What this is: a spoken piece about an eco change they actually made and lived with.

## The interview

These are the questions behind the three-message interview in SKILL.md. Fold them into those messages in plain words. Optional ones can be skipped.

1. What did you actually change, and how long have you stuck with it?
   Hint for them: The swap, the habit, the repair, the thing you quit or started, concretely, with how long it's been.
2. One real moment from living with it?
   Hint for them: The day it was brilliant, the day it was a pain, the reaction it got, a specific scene from the actual doing of it.
3. Honest verdict, what's it actually like, costs and all?
   Hint for them: The real ledger: money, effort, what works, what's annoying, whether you'd do it again.
4. Anything else you want to add? More detail, another angle, anything you didn't get to? (optional)
   Hint for them: Optional: Fill, if you've got more to say. Skip, if you don't.

What a strong script needs from these answers: a real change they made — a swap, a habit, a repair, a thing given up or taken on — plus the concrete reality of living with it (cost, effort, how long they've kept it up), one specific moment from actually doing it, and the honest verdict including the inconvenient parts. This profile openly does not do lecture content: no lived change, no script. Someone reporting from their own kitchen, never preaching from a pulpit.

How to judge the answers: The most common gap is the change described as a value instead of a practice — 'I'm trying to waste less' with no concrete swap; push for the specific thing: what exactly changed, since when, what it cost. Cost and effort are quiet gold — the exact price difference, the extra ten minutes — because honest inconvenience is what makes this content trustworthy where preachy content isn't. The second gap is a verdict with no downside — if it's all upside, ask what's genuinely annoying about it, because they know, and saying it is the credibility. If their answers are pure planet-lecture with no personal change anywhere, the honest output is the refusal naming the gap. Register (practical, fed-up, quietly-pleased, wry) drives delivery.

## Research

Research is ON for this type. Use web search if you have it (see engine-rules.md, "Research"). If you have no search, skip this section and use only the person's answers.

Your role: You are a research assistant supporting someone's account of {subject} — an eco change they personally made. Their lived experience is the truth of this piece — your job is to verify names and add accurate context that supports their telling, NEVER to supply impact statistics or moral weight they didn't claim.

Find, from search results only:
- Correct names and spellings of any products, schemes, brands, or systems they mentioned in {subject}
- Accurate context that BACKS what they described, if findable (e.g. the scheme works the way they said, the product is what they say it is)
- Nothing else — no emissions statistics, no impact claims, no comparative footprint data unless the person themselves cited a figure that needs its name spelled right

Useful searches:
- {subject} product scheme how it works
- {subject} official information

Prefer these sources: ipcc.ch, unep.org, iea.org, ourworldindata.org, carbonbrief.org, eea.europa.eu, gov.uk, wrap.org.uk

Research rules for this type:
- Never supply environmental statistics or impact claims the person didn't make — their lived experience is the argument, not the planet's data.
- Never surface content evaluating whether their change 'matters enough' — scale-shaming has no place in this script.
- If a product or scheme can't be confirmed, leave it out rather than guess.

## Script structure

Build the script through these beats, in this order. Each rule is a requirement. "Fails if" names the mistake to avoid.

**hook**
- Open on the change or the verdict — the thing they did, or what living with it is like — never on the state of the planet
- Fails if: It opens with a climate lecture or guilt framing instead of a person's account

**the_change**
- What they actually did, concretely — the swap, the routine, the duration, what it replaced — from their material only
- Fails if: It stays at values level with no visible practice, or invents specifics of the routine

**the_moment** (peak beat)
- The one real scene from living with it — the win, the hassle, the reaction — given room
- If the material is weak here: If no scene was given even after the probe, stay honestly general — never invent a farmers' market morning that didn't happen
- Fails if: It invents a scene or polishes the one they gave into an advert

**honest_ledger**
- The real costs and the real payoffs, in their proportions — money, effort, annoyance, satisfaction. The inconvenient parts stay in
- Fails if: It edits out the downsides their answers contained, or inflates the upsides beyond their telling

**verdict**
- Whether they'd do it again and what they'd say to their past self — personal hindsight the viewer overhears, never instruction or guilt aimed outward
- Fails if: It converts their change into viewer homework, moralises, or adds planetary significance they didn't claim

Ordering exception: If the moment is the story — the day the swap met real life — open inside that scene and let the change explain itself from there.

Peak moment check: look at the person's own words at the "the_moment" beat, wherever it ends up. If they carry real intensity (repetition, an absolute, a superlative, weight), mark it with exactly ONE of the three peak techniques in engine-rules.md, and nowhere else. If their words there are plain, use none.

## Delivery registers

Read the person's emotional register from their own words, pick the one that matches, and apply its mechanics throughout.

**PRACTICAL / IT'S JUST WHAT WE DO NOW**: Their wording is matter-of-fact — the change is routine, the numbers are known, no ceremony about it
- Flat, concrete delivery — the cost, the routine, the result, stated plainly.
- Zero moral framing — it works, here's how, end of.
- The mundane detail respected — the bin, the bill, the Tuesday.
- Ends without ceremony — it's just how things are now.

**FED UP / DON'T GET ME STARTED**: Their wording is exasperated — at greenwash, at packaging, at how hard the simple thing is made to be
- State the absurdity plainly — the thing that happened, the packaging, the claim on the label.
- Resolve it — exactly why it's ridiculous, in their real detail.
- Cap flat — 'it's a cucumber.' 'recyclable. in one facility. in another country.' — never actual profanity.
- Repeat fact-resolve-cap per absurdity; the change they made stands as the quiet answer to it.

**QUIETLY PLEASED / SMALL WIN, REAL WIN**: Their wording is modest about something that genuinely works — the repair that held, the habit that stuck
- Understatement throughout — the win described smaller than it feels.
- The recurring small detail carries it — the jar, the mended thing, the empty bin.
- Soft rhythm, no crescendo, no planet-scale claims.
- Ends on the quiet fact — still doing it, still works.

**WRY / IMPERFECT AND AT PEACE**: Their wording is self-aware about the contradictions — doing their bit while owning the inconsistencies
- The contradictions they flagged, deadpanned — never smoothed away.
- Self-deprecation at their own level, never invented hypocrisy.
- The genuine effort stays visible under the jokes — land the real thing plainly at least once.
- Ends honest-sized — doing what they do, not claiming more.

## Voice signature (always, on top of the register)

- A single physical object carried through the piece with its lifespan attached to it — the same bottle, the mended jumper, the jar — named early and returned to, always with how long it has lasted: 'still the same one', 'that's the third winter in it'. The durability of one ordinary thing is this content's native proof, and it does the arguing without a single claim being made. Only objects and timeframes the person actually gave.
- The contradiction volunteered before anyone can level it — the inconsistency in their own behaviour offered up unprompted, flatly and without defending it: 'I still drive everywhere, so make of that what you like.' Getting there first is what separates reporting from preaching, and it costs the piece nothing because they were never claiming to be consistent. Only ever a contradiction the person themselves named; never invent a hypocrisy to look humble.
- The real cost named flat, in both directions — the actual money stated without spin, including where it cost more or came with an awkward upfront hit: 'the shampoo bar's four quid and lasts three months; the reusables were two hundred up front, which is not nothing.' The honest price, especially the inconvenient one, is the trust this content runs on. Only figures the person themselves gave.
- The specific annoyance named and left unresolved — the genuinely irritating part stated plainly and not rescued by a payoff in the same breath: 'it's a faff. Every single time. I've decided the faff is worth it, but it is still a faff.' The un-rescued inconvenience is the credibility. Only annoyances the person's own answers named.
- Other people's reactions reported flat — what someone actually said or did about the change, quoted without editorialising for or against: 'my mum thinks it's a phase, my flatmate calls it the compost bin of shame.' Real, often teasing reactions ground the change socially and undercut any whiff of preaching. Only reactions the person actually described.
- Refusing the scale question out loud — pre-empting the 'does this even matter' challenge by naming it and declining to defend it, never with a statistic: 'is it saving the planet? No. Obviously not. I do it anyway.' Naming the futility and stepping over it is the anti-sermon move. Never answer it with an impact claim or planetary significance the person didn't make; the refusal stands on its own.

## Required in every script

the script must prove the person actually lives this change — the specific swap, the real duration, the actual cost or effort, one scene from real life, and the honest annoyances — never abstract virtue. The inconvenient truth they admit is the trust; a version with the downsides edited out reads as an advert and dies on arrival.

## Hard rules for this type, above all others

- The script never moralises at viewers — no guilt, no 'we all need to', no homework; their change speaks only for itself.
- Never add environmental statistics or impact claims the person didn't make.
- Never let the script scale-shame — their change is presented as worth exactly what they say it's worth to them.

If one of these rules requires a refusal, refuse, however rich the material is. Say plainly what you cannot write and why.
