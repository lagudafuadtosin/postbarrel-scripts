# Behind the scenes

What this is: a spoken piece showing how something is really made or done, or the person who does it, read by the owner over their own footage — the real process, never a staged tour.

## The interview

These are the questions behind the three-message interview in SKILL.md. Fold them into those messages in plain words. Optional ones can be skipped.

1. What's the process or the person you're showing?
   Hint for them: One thing being made or done, or one specific person doing their work.
2. Walk me through one real part of it, the step people don't see?
   Hint for them: Concrete and specific. The actual thing your hands do, not a summary.
3. What's the fiddly, boring, or hard bit most people wouldn't guess?
   Hint for them: The unglamorous truth: the wait, the mess, the repetition, the thing that goes wrong. This is what makes it real.
4. Whose hands is this? You, a specific person, or the team? (optional)
   Hint for them: First name or role is plenty. Keep it to what you'd happily say on camera.
5. Anything else you want to add? More detail, another angle, anything you didn't get to? (optional)
   Hint for them: Optional: Fill, if you've got more to say. Skip, if you don't.

What a strong script needs from these answers: one real step of an actual process (or one real person doing one real thing), and the un-glamorous part most people wouldn't guess — the fiddly, slow, or boring bit that proves it's real. Credibility comes from keeping the unglamorous detail in, not from a montage. The staged 'meet our amazing passionate team' reel is the failure mode; a real one shows one concrete step, one specific pair of hands, and the graft nobody sees.

How to judge the answers: The most common gap is a summary instead of a step — 'we make it with care' rather than the actual thing the hands do. Push for one concrete, watchable step. The second gap is the polish reflex: owners hide the unglamorous part, but the fiddly/slow/boring bit is exactly the proof the piece needs — surface it and keep it in. If a specific person does the work, get the first name or role; a real pair of hands beats 'the team' every time. Register (craft pride, the graft, one person's hands, this is normal for us) drives delivery.

## Research

Research is OFF for this type. Nothing can be verified, so write only from the person's answers.

## Script structure

Build the script through these beats, in this order. Each rule is a requirement. "Fails if" names the mistake to avoid.

**the_setup**
- What we're looking at, plainly — the process or the person named in the owner's own words, no grand framing
- Fails if: It opens with a montage-style boast ('meet our incredible team') instead of naming the one concrete thing on screen

**the_real_step** (peak beat)
- The actual step, told concretely — the specific thing the hands do, in real time, watchable rather than summarised
- If the material is weak here: If the step was given only in summary, hold it to the one concrete action that was described rather than inventing the missing detail
- Fails if: It summarises the process ('made with care') instead of showing one real, specific step

**the_unseen**
- The fiddly, slow, boring, or hard bit the customer never sees — the graft that proves the step is real, kept in rather than polished out
- If the material is weak here: If no unglamorous detail was given, stay with the plain reality of the step rather than manufacturing drama or difficulty
- Fails if: It smooths the process into effortless perfection, or invents a hardship the person didn't describe

**close**
- A plain landing in the owner's register — the pride is in the doing and stays unspoken, never a sudden sell or a slogan
- Fails if: It ends on a slogan, a 'that's why we're the best', or a call to action the piece never earned

Ordering exception: If the unglamorous bit is the most striking thing, it can open — the wait, the mess, the repetition — and let the step it belongs to follow.

Peak moment check: look at the person's own words at the "the_real_step" beat, wherever it ends up. If they carry real intensity (repetition, an absolute, a superlative, weight), mark it with exactly ONE of the three peak techniques in engine-rules.md, and nowhere else. If their words there are plain, use none.

## Delivery registers

Read the person's emotional register from their own words, pick the one that matches, and apply its mechanics throughout.

**CRAFT PRIDE / THE CARE IS IN THE DOING**: Their wording carries quiet pride in doing the step properly — attention, standard, care
- Narrate the step in the present, in real time — the hands doing the thing.
- Let the care show through the specificity, never through the word 'passion'.
- The unglamorous detail is offered as part of the pride, not despite it.
- Ends on the plain doing, no flourish.

**THE GRAFT / HARDER THAN IT LOOKS**: Their wording owns that it's tougher, slower, or messier than the finished thing suggests
- Lead into the effort — the time it takes, the bit that fights back.
- Keep the mess and the repetition in; that's the whole point.
- No self-pity — it's just the work, stated flat.
- Ends on the graft as ordinary, matter-of-fact.

**ONE PERSON'S HANDS / THIS IS [NAME]'S JOB**: A specific person does this step, and the owner named them
- Put the named person at the centre — first name or role, as given.
- Show the one thing those hands are good at, concretely.
- Keep it human-scale — one person, one task, no 'our talented team'.
- Ends on the person, plainly, no tribute.

**THIS IS NORMAL FOR US / JUST HOW IT'S DONE**: Their wording treats the impressive step as everyday — routine to them, surprising to an outsider
- Deliver the striking step as matter-of-fact routine.
- Let the audience be impressed; the owner isn't trying to impress.
- The unglamorous detail lands as 'just Tuesday', not a reveal.
- Ends flat and unbothered, the ordinary made quietly interesting.

## Voice signature (always, on top of the register)

- The unglamorous detail kept in on purpose — the four-hour wait, the third attempt, the mess to clean up, the sheer repetition — offered plainly as part of the work rather than edited out, because the boring truth is exactly what a staged montage can't fake. Only ever the un-glamour the person actually described.
- The step narrated in the present, in real time — 'this goes in, then it sits, then we…' — the hands shown doing the thing rather than the process summarised after the fact. Only the step the person gave, held to its real detail.
- One specific tool, timing, temperature, or number carrying the reality — the actual thing that makes it this process and not a generic one — stated as a plain fact. Only the specific the person provided.
- A named pair of hands over an abstract team — a first name or a role doing one real thing, human-scale, never 'our talented, passionate team'. Only the person the owner named, at the level they gave.
- The pride left unspoken and carried by the doing — the care shows in how exactly the step is described, never in the word 'passion', 'love', or 'dedication' stated outright.

## Required in every script

the script must prove this is a real process really being done — one concrete, watchable step and the unglamorous part kept in — never a montage of effortless perfection. Polish and 'passionate team' language are the failure mode; the specific step and the graft nobody sees are the trust.

## Hard rules for this type, above all others

- Never invent a step, a tool, a timing, or a person the owner didn't give — a fabricated process is the exact opposite of what 'behind the scenes' is for.
- Never manufacture hardship or drama the person didn't describe — the graft is only credible when it's the real graft; invented difficulty reads as fake as invented perfection.
- Keep any individual at the identity level the owner gave — first name or role only, never a full name, and never a detail that identifies a staff member who didn't consent to it.
- Never claim a standard, certification, hygiene rating, or regulated practice unless the person's own answers stated it; research is disabled and nothing here can be verified.
- Never disparage a competitor or imply theirs is the sloppy version; the process stands on its own.

If one of these rules requires a refusal, refuse, however rich the material is. Say plainly what you cannot write and why.

## Business details

Before the interview, ask once for a short description of the business (what it is, where, what it sells). Treat it as established fact about who they are. Reuse it for later scripts in the same chat.

## Moves for this type

Use from the Moves list in engine-rules.md: 3, 6, 7, 8, 9, 11. Only where their material supports them.
Never: 1 as hype, 12 against a competitor, 3 unless they gave the hard part.

Default length: short (see the length bands in engine-rules.md).
