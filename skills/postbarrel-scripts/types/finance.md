# Money

What this is: a spoken piece about something they actually did with their money.

This type can cover several items in one script (for example "my top 3"). If the person wants more than one, ask for the overall topic first, then ask the questions below once per item, and use the multi-item rules in engine-rules.md.

## The interview

These are the questions behind the three-message interview in SKILL.md. Fold them into those messages in plain words. Optional ones can be skipped.

1. What did you actually do with your money?
   Hint for them: The thing that happened, the save, the spend, the debt, the experiment, the mistake. Your story, not a concept.
2. Give me the receipt, the real numbers.
   Hint for them: The figure, the timeframe, what it cost or made, the before and after. As exact as you're comfortable sharing.
3. What would you tell the you from before this?
   Hint for them: The lesson in your own framing, what you'd repeat, what you'd never do again, what surprised you.

What a strong script needs from these answers: a real money thing they did — saved, spent, invested, got out of debt, made a mistake, tried a method — plus at least one receipt (an exact figure, a timeframe, a named account/product/outcome) and the lesson as THEY frame it. This profile openly does not do guru-explainer content: no receipt from their own life, no script. The person is a peer sharing what happened, never an advisor.

How to judge the answers: The whole profile stands on the receipt — if q_receipt is vague ('I saved a lot'), that's the follow-up: the actual number, the actual months, the actual account or product. Without at least one real figure the script has no spine. The second gap is a win with the mess edited out — if it sounds frictionless, ask what went wrong along the way or what it cost them, because the mistake is usually the most valuable material. If their answers are concept-teaching with no personal action anywhere ('people should budget better'), the honest output is the refusal naming the gap: this profile needs something they did. Register (candid, cautionary, chuffed, contrarian) drives delivery.

## Research

This type NEEDS web search. The facts are the video, and a wrong fact here can mislead or harm a viewer or wrong a real person. If you have no web search, do not interview and do not write. Say only: "This type needs web search to check facts. Turn search on and ask again."

With search, research is ON (see engine-rules.md, "Research").

Your role: You are a research assistant supporting someone's account of {subject} — a money thing they personally did. Their experience and numbers are the truth of this piece — your job is to verify names and add accurate context that supports their telling, NEVER to check, correct, or evaluate their personal figures or choices.

Find, from search results only:
- Correct names and spellings of any account types, products, schemes, or institutions they mentioned in {subject}
- Accurate general context that BACKS their framing, if findable (e.g. the scheme they used exists and works as they describe, the timeframe they mention matches how the product works)
- Nothing else — no rates, no projections, no advice content

Useful searches:
- {subject} official information
- {subject} how it works

Prefer these sources: reuters.com, bloomberg.com, ft.com, cnbc.com, marketwatch.com, investopedia.com, sec.gov, bankofengland.co.uk, ons.gov.uk

Research rules for this type:
- Never supply figures the person didn't give — their numbers are the only numbers in this script.
- Never surface content that evaluates their choice as good or bad — the verdict is theirs.
- If a product or scheme they named can't be confirmed, leave it out rather than guess.

## Script structure

Build the script through these beats, in this order. Each rule is a requirement. "Fails if" names the mistake to avoid.

**hook**
- Open on the receipt or the claim — the number, or the thing they did — never on throat-clearing about money being important
- Fails if: It opens like a finance channel intro instead of a person's story, or leads with advice framing

**the_thing**
- What they actually did, as a story — the situation before, the decision, what happened — from their material only
- Fails if: It drifts into explaining concepts instead of telling what they did, or invents steps they didn't describe

**the_receipt** (peak beat)
- The numbers, exact and bare — the figure, the timeframe, the before/after — stated plainly, given room to land
- If the material is weak here: If they gave a range or an approximation, keep it at exactly that precision — never sharpen 'a few grand' into a figure
- Fails if: It invents, rounds, or sharpens any number beyond what the person stated

**the_mess**
- What it actually cost or where it went wrong — the mistake, the friction, the part the highlight version leaves out
- Fails if: It reads as a frictionless win with no honest cost, when their answers contained one

**the_lesson**
- What they'd tell their past self — in THEIR framing, as personal hindsight, never as instruction to viewers
- Fails if: It converts their lesson into advice, steps, or shoulds aimed at the audience, or adds a lesson they didn't draw

Ordering exception: If the number is the story — the debt figure, the total saved — open on the bare number before any context, and let the story explain it.

Peak moment check: look at the person's own words at the "the_receipt" beat, wherever it ends up. If they carry real intensity (repetition, an absolute, a superlative, weight), mark it with exactly ONE of the three peak techniques in engine-rules.md, and nowhere else. If their words there are plain, use none.

## Delivery registers

Read the person's emotional register from their own words, pick the one that matches, and apply its mechanics throughout.

**CANDID / RECEIPTS ON THE TABLE**: Their wording is open and flat about the numbers — no shame, no bragging, just what happened
- Numbers stated bare, as their own short sentences — 'Twelve grand. Four years.'
- Peer register throughout — talking across, never down.
- The uncomfortable figure said plainly does the work — no cushioning before it, no justifying after.
- Ends level — what happened, what they know now, done.

**CAUTIONARY / LEARN FROM MY MESS**: Their wording centres the mistake — the thing they'd undo, the expensive lesson
- Tell the mistake with the logic they had at the time — why it made sense then, so the fall lands honestly.
- The cost stated exactly, once, flat.
- Self-directed rue, never self-flagellation — 'past me was so sure.'
- The lesson lands as hindsight to their own past self, which the viewer overhears — never as a lecture.

**CHUFFED / IT ACTUALLY WORKED**: Their wording carries quiet or loud pride — the milestone hit, the debt gone, the thing that compounded
- Build to the number like a reveal — the grind first, then the figure, short and bare.
- The win sized honestly at their scale — a first £500 saved treated with the same respect as a paid-off mortgage.
- The cost and the timeframe stay visible — earned, not lucky.
- Pride without preaching — the script never turns and tells the viewer to do it too.

**CONTRARIAN / EVERYONE SAYS X, I DID Y**: Their wording pushes against standard advice — the rule they broke, the conventional wisdom that failed them
- State the conventional wisdom plainly, then what they actually did, then the receipt.
- The evidence is the argument — their real outcome versus the standard script.
- Confidence bounded by their own case — 'this worked for me' energy, stated as their experience, never universalised.
- Cap with the number, not a manifesto.

## Voice signature (always, on top of the register)

- The flinch, owned rather than softened — naming the figure that is embarrassing to say out loud and refusing to cushion it: 'I had 12000 on a credit card. Twelve grand. On a card.' The discomfort stays audible instead of being managed away, which is what makes the receipt land. Only ever their own stated figure, at their own precision — the flinch is in the delivery, never in a number adjusted to make the point sharper.
- Explicitly declining the advice turn at the exact moment the format expects it — the beat where money content normally pivots to instruction, named and not taken: 'I'm not telling you to do this', 'this worked for me and that is the whole claim'. This is how a person talks about their own money without pretending to be an adviser; it is a voice move, not a disclaimer bolted on the end, and it never appears as a formal warning.
- Numbered, structured walkthrough as a delivery option — when the person's material is genuinely a sequence of steps or accounts/categories, present it as a clean numbered list delivered plainly, one item landing before the next begins, rather than blending them into flowing prose.
- One real figure as the anchor the whole piece hangs on — a single number named early and returned to, the thing the account is actually about: the debt, the price paid, the amount that came back. Everything else orients around that one figure. Only their exact number, at their precision, never rounded or sharpened.
- The feeling on the money owned plainly, across the arc — the real emotion attached to the figure stated without performance: the dread before, the relief after, the stupidity admitted in hindsight. The turn in how it felt, landed flat and never converted into a lesson for the viewer. Only the person's own stated feeling.

## Required in every script

the script must carry at least one real receipt — an exact figure, a real timeframe, a named product or outcome — from the person's own life, at exactly the precision they gave. Their numbers are the entire authority of this content: never invented, never rounded, never sharpened, never supplemented with researched figures. A money story with no number is a failure; a money story with an invented number is worse. A blunt, confident delivery style is fine and matches how real people discuss money — but confidence in delivery must never be read as license to state something as a recommendation. 'This worked for me' stays clearly separate from 'you should do this.'

## Hard rules for this type, above all others

- The script is one person's account, never financial advice — no instructions, steps, or recommendations to viewers, no 'you should', and never any claim about what will happen for someone else.
- Never invent, round, sharpen, or supplement the person's figures — their stated numbers are the only numbers.
- Never name or promote specific investments as opportunities — products appear only as part of what the person did, at their framing.

If one of these rules requires a refusal, refuse, however rich the material is. Say plainly what you cannot write and why.
